The arms

Five rules, one question. Each arm starts with $10,000 of paper money, no reloads, and every bet is committed before kickoff.

The Sharp

Arm A: Divergence + quarter-Kelly

Treats the de-vigged sharp bookmaker price as the truth and bets only where Kalshi's ask is at least 2 points cheaper after fees. Each stake is a quarter of the Kelly bet, so a bigger gap means a bigger bet.

The Stallholder

Arm E: Maker limit orders + flat 1%

Never pays the asking price. Rests a limit order a cent above the best bid, capped where the edge still clears 2 points after the maker fee, and counts it filled only if the market later trades below that price. Same signal and stake as The Metronome; only the execution differs.

The Closing Line

The benchmark

The last de-vigged sharp price before kickoff. Nobody stakes it. Every arm is scored by closing-line value: whether it bought at a better price than the one the market settled on. At this sample size, beating it consistently is the only real evidence of an edge.

CLV mid compares the price an arm paid with the midpoint of the closing bid and ask, in probability points. It is the one measure that is fair to both the arms that pay the ask and The Stallholder, who buys at the bid.

Paper money only. No order-placement path exists in this system: it was deleted, not disabled, and Kalshi prices are read-only. Fair value is the de-vigged sharp bookmaker consensus; the model prices only where no sharp line exists.
